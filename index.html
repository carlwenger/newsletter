Daily News Report

Sunday, October 11, 2026

  • A new report from the White House Council of Economic Advisers says the country has added 72,000 manufacturing jobs so far in 2026, many of them in durable goods such as metal products and transportation equipment. That reverses the last two years of the prior administration, when about 200,000 factory jobs disappeared. The advisers credit the President's push to bring production home, his tariff policies, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which lets manufacturers immediately deduct 100 percent of the cost of new factories, machinery and equipment. For working families, that means more steady, well-paying blue-collar jobs. [1]
  • President Trump spoke at a large rally in Clarksville, Tennessee, on Saturday, campaigning for Senator Bill Hagerty and for Marsha Blackburn's run for governor ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. He highlighted billions of dollars in new manufacturing investment in the state and his administration's efforts to fight crime, and he spoke about the tensions with Iran. Musician Kid Rock opened the event with a faith-centered introduction, defending religious freedom and saying America is and always will be one nation under God. The rally was part of a busy fall schedule meant to keep both houses of Congress in Republican hands. [2]
  • The President named Katie Bailey as the new director of the White House Office of Legislative Affairs, the team that works directly with Congress to pass his agenda. Bailey accepted the promotion on Friday. She had been serving as deputy director and brings long Capitol Hill experience, including six years as legislative director for then-Senator Mike Braun of Indiana and earlier work for the late Senator Tom Coburn and the House Oversight Committee. She replaces James Braid, who left in September. Her appointment keeps an experienced hand in charge of negotiations with lawmakers in the weeks before the midterms. [3]
  • The Justice Department's Antitrust Division is investigating whether the five networks that make up the White House television pool broke federal antitrust law when they jointly stopped covering the start of the President's summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping last month. The networks were told on Friday to turn over communications and materials related to the pool. A department spokesperson explained that group boycotts among business competitors can violate the Sherman Act, the 1890 law protecting free competition. The probe asks whether major networks coordinated to deny the public coverage of an important diplomatic event. [4]
  • Secretary of State Marco Rubio strongly condemned a Houthi missile attack on Saturday that struck two domestic terminals at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. At least a dozen people were killed, including an American citizen, and more than 300 were injured. Rubio called the attacks reprehensible, said they must stop now, and declared that the United States stands with Saudi Arabia. Asked whether America might join Saudi strikes against the Iran-backed group, President Trump said, "We may," adding that he would speak with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and that the administration moves very quickly. [5]
  • President Trump said it is time for Ukraine to choose a new leader who can make a peace deal, saying President Zelenskyy could have reached many agreements but never does. The President noted that Ukraine has lost about 30 percent of its territory and that more than two million people on both sides have been killed in a war he says would never have started on his watch. He also said he had warned Ukraine not to strike Russian refineries, because that drives up diesel costs worldwide and hurts American farmers and ranchers. The remarks came after Zelenskyy criticized a U.S. diesel arrangement with Russia. [6]
  • President Trump responded to being passed over for the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize by listing what his administration has achieved. In a Truth Social post, he said he has settled eight wars and is close to ending two more, brought home all of the Israeli hostages including the final 28, secured the release of hundreds of other hostages from around the world, won in Venezuela and captured its dictator, and stopped Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon. The President has been nominated for the prize at least five times, including by former Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. [7]
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed back hard against Senator Elizabeth Warren in a letter he posted on X. Warren has sent Treasury more than 100 letters over 21 months, and her latest blamed the administration for higher borrowing costs. Bessent rejected that claim and reminded her that interest rates climbed to their highest level in 22 years and inflation hit a 40-year high while she cheered on the Biden administration's spending. He said it was difficult to treat her concerns as sincere. The exchange highlights the administration's argument that today's affordability problems trace back to the previous administration's policies. [8]
  • Judicial Watch President Tom Fitton is pressing the FBI to speed up the release of about 75,000 pages of records on Thomas Matthew Crooks, who tried to assassinate President Trump at a rally in Butler, Pennsylvania, in July 2024. Fitton said that at the current pace the release would take 12 years. Judicial Watch sued the Justice Department in 2025 after its records request was rejected, and the lawsuit has already forced out several batches, including text messages Crooks sent to a person whose name the FBI blacked out. Americans still deserve full answers about how the shooting happened. [9]
  • President Trump said he will make the final decision on whether the firing squad execution of Fort Hood gunman Nidal Hasan, scheduled for December 3, will be livestreamed. Hasan, a former Army major, was convicted of murdering 13 people and wounding 32 others at Fort Hood in 2009. The President earlier approved an Army firing squad on the recommendation of War Secretary Pete Hegseth, and this will be the military's first execution in more than six decades. He said there are arguments both ways, but that showing it might deter others from committing similar attacks. [10]
